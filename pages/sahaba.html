AL Sahaba

A exploration into the lives of those great souls who recognized the
truth of the message in its earliest dawn, laying the foundations of the
Ummah with unwavering faith

Khadijah bint Khuwaylid

The Pure One

When the Prophet ﷺ returned from the first revelation deeply shaken, Khadijah comforted him and affirmed his noble character. She then took him to Waraqah ibn Nawfal, whose response helped provide early confirmation that the experience was connected to the tradition of previous prophets.

READ MORE

Abu Bakr As-Siddiq

As-Siddiq

After the Prophet ﷺ died, many Muslims were overwhelmed by grief. Abu Bakr addressed the community and affirmed that Muhammad ﷺ had died, while Allah remains Ever-Living. He recited Qur'an 3:144, helping restore clarity and steadiness at one of the most difficult moments in early Islamic history.

READ MORE

Aisha bint Abi Bakr

The Truthful
daughter of the Truthful

During the incident of al-Ifk, false accusations caused profound hardship within the Muslim community. The Qur'an later declared her innocence in Surah al-Nur, establishing principles concerning evidence, false accusation, and the responsibility of believers when confronted with harmful rumours.

READ MORE

Umar ibn Al-Khattab

Al-Faruq

After accepting Islam, Umar did not migrate in secrecy like many Muslims. Instead, he openly announced his departure from Makkah, challenging anyone who wished to prevent him. He performed tawaf at the Kaaba and prayed openly before leaving. Despite the hostility of Quraysh, no one dared to stop him, reflecting his strength and the growing respect he commanded even among his opponents.

READ MORE

Uthman ibn Affan

Dhun-Nurayn

During his caliphate, differences in Qur'anic recitation became increasingly visible as Islam spread. Uthman ordered standardized copies to be prepared from the manuscript preserved from the earlier compilation and sent to major regions, helping protect the community from division over the written text.

READ MORE

Ali ibn Abi Talib

Abu Turab

On the night of the Hijrah, Ali remained behind in Makkah while the Prophet ﷺ departed. He later returned property entrusted to the Prophet ﷺ to its owners, demonstrating both courage and trustworthiness during a dangerous moment in the early Muslim mission.

READ MORE

Bilal ibn Ribah

The Prophrt's Muezzin

Bilal was tortured in Makkah because of his faith. Under severe persecution, he remained steadfast in his belief in Allah’s oneness. Abu Bakr later purchased his freedom, and Bilal became one of the most recognizable voices of the Muslim community through the adhan.

READ MORE

Mus'ab ibn Umayr

The Ambassador of Islam

After the First Pledge of Aqabah, the Prophet ﷺ sent Mus'ab to Yathrib. He taught the Qur'an and called people to Islam, and through his efforts prominent figures, including Usayd ibn Hudayr and Sa'd ibn Mu'adh accepted Islam, strengthening the foundations of the Muslim community before the Hijrah.

READ MORE

Julaybib

The Beloved Sahabe

Julaybib was later found among those killed in battle after having fought against several opponents. The Prophet ﷺ searched for him, found him among the fallen, and said, “He is from me, and I am from him,” before personally taking responsibility for his burial.

READ MORE

Mu'awiya ibn Abu Sufyan

1st Caliph of the Umayyad Caliphate

After the death of Ali and the abdication of al-Hasan ibn Ali Mu'awiyah’s rule became broadly recognized, bringing an end to a major phase of internal conflict. The year became known as the Year of Unity because political authority was reunited under one ruler.

They shared his journey, supported his mission, and devoted themselves to the message he brought.
